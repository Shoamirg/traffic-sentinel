    Problem & approach

    Input: one fixed road camera. Output A: traffic events as [start_sec, end_sec, label] across 14 classes. Output B: a causal per-frame accident risk in [0, 1] that should cross 0.5 up to 5 s before an accident, using only past frames.

    Learnedweights or statistics fitted to data Rule-basedhand-written logic with named thresholds Hand-drawnper-camera geometry

    Models & data

    • YOLO11m (COCO-pretrained) for person, car, bus, truck, motorcycle, bicycle and animals at 1280 px, ~10 analysed fps.
    • Fire/smoke detector fine-tuned on public fire/smoke imagery. TODO: dataset name + size.
    • Scene model: a 48 × 27 grid learned from vehicle tracks - visit counts (road mask), 8-bin heading histograms (lane directions), dwell time (queue zones). A prior from the sample videos is blended with the test clip's own tracks.
    • Signal state: lamp brightness in a drawn ROI, on/off threshold chosen per video with Otsu.

    Why this design

    • Fourteen classes and a handful of labelled clips: far too little to train an end-to-end event model, but plenty to learn where and which way traffic flows.
    • Rules over tracks are debuggable: every false positive can be traced to one threshold in config.py.
    • Learned directions make wrong-way and U-turn logic camera-agnostic instead of drawn per scene.
    • Part B is kept separate so it can never peek at future frames or at Part A's offline output.

    Rebuild it in five steps

    1. Sample frames to ~10 fps at 1280 px width; detect with YOLO11m (conf 0.25).
    2. Track with two-stage (high/low confidence) IoU association and constant-velocity prediction; drop tracks under 5 observations; smooth positions over 0.6 s.
    3. Accumulate the scene grid from moving vehicles (> 25 px/s) and cells with ≥ 3 visits.
    4. Run the 14 per-class rules below over tracks + scene + layout.
    5. Merge segments closer than 1.0 s; drop segments shorter than 0.5 s.
